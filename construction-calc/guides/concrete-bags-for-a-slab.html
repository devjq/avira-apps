How many bags of concrete for a 10x10 slab?

A 10-by-10-foot slab 4 inches thick holds 33.33 cubic feet of concrete, or 1.23 cubic yards. With no waste that takes 56 bags of 80-lb mix, or 75 bags of 60-lb mix. Add 10% for waste and it takes 62 bags of 80 lb or 82 bags of 60 lb. The bag counts use the yields printed on Quikrete's data sheet, 0.60 cu ft for an 80-lb bag and 0.45 cu ft for a 60-lb bag, and round up to whole bags.

Bags per cubic yard

A cubic yard is 27 cubic feet. Divide 27 by the bag's yield. Yields vary by brand and mix, so read the bag and use the yield printed on it.

Worked example: 10 x 10 x 4 in, 10% waste

  1. Volume. 10 ft × 10 ft × (4 ÷ 12) ft = 33.33 cu ft.
  2. Waste. 33.33 × 1.10 = 36.67 cu ft.
  3. Cubic yards. 36.67 ÷ 27 = 1.36 cu yd.
  4. 80-lb bags. 36.67 ÷ 0.60 = 61.12, round up to 62 bags.
  5. 60-lb bags. 36.67 ÷ 0.45 = 81.49, round up to 82 bags.

The steps use the rounded 36.67. Using the exact 110/3 cubic feet gives 61.11 and 81.48, which round to the same bag counts.

Notes

  • Why round up. You can't buy part of a bag, so every count goes up to the next whole bag.
  • Waste. 10% is the default in Shim's concrete tool, which accepts 0% to 50%. The allowance covers spillage, an uneven subgrade and over-excavation.
  • Not included. Thickened edges, footings and keyways. Figure each one as length × width × depth and add it to the slab.
  • Premixed concrete only. The yields are for concrete mix. Gravel, sand and mortar mixes have their own yields.
  • Ready-mix. If you order by the cubic yard, use the cubic-yard column, and check the supplier's minimum load.

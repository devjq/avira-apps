How many bags of concrete per fence post?

A 4x4 post set in a 10-inch-diameter hole, 30 inches deep, takes 1.15 cubic feet of concrete. That is 2 bags of 80-lb mix, or 3 bags of 60-lb mix. The post sits on the bottom of the hole, so the concrete is the hole's volume minus the post's volume. A 4x4 is 3-1/2 × 3-1/2 inches in actual size.

Concrete per post

Hole volume = π × (hole diameter ÷ 2)² × depth. Post volume = post width × post thickness × depth. Concrete per post = hole volume − post volume. Bags round up per post. The 50-lb column is not in Shim.

Worked example: 4x4 post, 10-inch hole, 30 inches deep

  1. Radius. 10 in ÷ 2 = 5 in.
  2. Hole volume. π × 5² × 30 = 2,356.19 cu in.
  3. Post volume. 3.5 × 3.5 × 30 = 367.50 cu in.
  4. Concrete. 2,356.19 − 367.50 = 1,988.69 cu in.
  5. Cubic feet. 1,988.69 ÷ 1,728 = 1.15 cu ft.
  6. 80-lb bags. 1.15 ÷ 0.60 = 1.92, round up to 2 bags.
  7. 60-lb bags. 1.15 ÷ 0.45 = 2.56, round up to 3 bags.

Notes

  • Shim's round-column tool. Enter the hole's diameter and depth, 10 in and 30 in, with waste set to 0%. It gives 1.36 cu ft for the hole. Subtract the post, 0.21 cu ft (3-1/2 × 3-1/2 × 30 in), to get 1.15 cu ft.
  • Gravel base. A gravel base shortens the concrete. With 4 inches of gravel and the post standing on it, the concrete fills the top 26 inches: (hole area − post area) × 26 in. For a 4x4 in a 10-inch hole that is 1.00 cu ft, still 2 bags of 80 lb or 3 bags of 60 lb.
  • Many posts: multiply first, round once. Six 4x4 posts in 60-lb bags need 6 × 1.15 = 6.9 cu ft, or 15.3 bags, so 16 bags. Rounding each post first would give 3 bags × 6 posts = 18 bags.
  • Hole size and depth are not set here. Frost depth, hole size and the post's design come from your local code and the plans. This page computes the volume only.
  • Yields. The bag counts use the yields in the slab guide, which are for premixed concrete mix.

How many steps for a 9-foot ceiling?

For a 9-foot ceiling over 2x10 floor joists, the total rise is 118-1/2 inches (9' 10-1/2"), not 108 inches. The total rise runs from the finished floor below to the finished floor above, so it includes the floor framing, the 3/4-inch subfloor and the 1/2-inch ceiling drywall. That takes 16 risers at 7-7/16 inches each, 15 treads, and a total run of 12' 6" at 10-inch treads. The numbers assume 2x10 joists, 3/4-inch subfloor, 1/2-inch drywall and no finish flooring on top.

Table 2: treads, run and stringer length

Treads = risers − 1. Total run = treads × tread depth. Stringer length is the diagonal from the bottom of the rise to the end of the run, √(total rise² + total run²). Tread depth is at least 10 inches (IRC R311.7.5.2), so the table shows 10-inch and 11-inch treads.

Worked example: 9-foot ceiling, 2x10 floor framing

  1. Ceiling height. 9 ft × 12 = 108 in.
  2. Joists. A 2x10 is 9-1/4 in deep.
  3. Total rise. 108 + 9-1/4 + 3/4 + 1/2 = 118-1/2 in (9' 10-1/2").
  4. Risers. 118.5 ÷ 7.75 = 15.29, round up to 16 risers.
  5. Riser height. 118.5 ÷ 16 = 7.41 in, shown as 7-7/16 in.
  6. Treads. 16 − 1 = 15 treads.
  7. Total run. 15 × 10 in = 150 in = 12' 6".
  8. Stringer length. √(118.5² + 150²) = 191.16 in = 15' 11-3/16".

The riser height is rounded to 1/16 inch. Sixteen risers at 7-7/16 inches add up to 119 inches, half an inch more than the 118-1/2-inch rise, so don't step off all 16 at the rounded number. The exact height is 7-13/32 inches (118-1/2 ÷ 16), which Shim shows at its 1/32-inch setting. On a 1/16-inch tape, alternate 7-3/8 and 7-7/16 inches, eight of each, which also adds up to 118-1/2 inches. The IRC allows up to 3/8 inch between the tallest and the shortest riser in a flight (R311.7.5.1), so a 1/16-inch alternation is fine, but leaving the whole half inch on one riser is not.

How to lay out a stair stringer

  1. Measure the real total rise at the opening, from the finished floor below to the finished floor above.
  2. Divide it into risers: total rise ÷ number of risers. Use the fewest risers that keep each one at or under 7-3/4 inches.
  3. Set the framing square to the riser height on one leg and the tread depth on the other, and step off the stringer.
  4. Cut the bottom riser shorter by the thickness of the tread, so the finished risers come out equal.

Notes

  • Finish flooring changes the rise. Tile, wood or carpet adds thickness to the finished floor. Measure to the finished surfaces, or add the finish thickness to the rise.
  • Local codes vary. The rules here are the 2021 International Residential Code. Your city or county may have amended them, and the permit set and the inspector govern.
  • Not covered: landings, winders, headroom and handrails.
